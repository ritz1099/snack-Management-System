Snack Counter Automation

Design document · Offices: Oslo & Bengaluru · Combined reporting currency: INR

1. The refill lifecycle — empty → reimbursed

A refill moves through six states. At any moment exactly one volunteer owns it; fallbacks reassign automatically on timeout.

1 · EMPTYAnyone reports the box empty via chat. A Refill record is created (id, office, timestamp).
2 · ASSIGNEDAssignment service picks exactly one eligible volunteer; chat asks them to refill by a deadline. Only one volunteer holds the refill.
3 · COMMITTEDThe volunteer accepts in chat. Purchase deadline set. No acceptance within the commit timeout → reassign to next.
4 · PURCHASEDVolunteer buys, refills, uploads receipt + enters amount / currency / snack description. Budget check runs, then a reimbursement claim is auto-filed to HR (amount, currency, description, receipt, employee id). No purchase by deadline → reassign.
5 · APPROVEDHR approves (1–10 days).
6 · REIMBURSEDHR confirms payment. Lifecycle closed.

If no eligible volunteer exists, all volunteers + the office lead are notified (escalation).

2. Worked example

Two offices, one refill each, ending in the combined monthly report (in INR).

Example A — Oslo box (NOK budget 6 000/month)

Aman clicks “Report box empty (Oslo)” in chat.
Bot (public): “Oslo box empty. Refill #142.”
Assignment: eligible = volunteers currently in Oslo; Priya chosen (fewest refills, longest since last). ASSIGNED
Bot → Priya: “Can you refill Oslo box #142 by Wed? [Accept] [Decline]”
Priya clicks Accept. COMMITTED
Priya buys snacks, refills, uploads receipt, types “340 NOK, oat bars”.
Budget check: Oslo month-to-date 5 700 NOK + 340 = 6 040 NOK ≤ 6 000? Over by 40 → flagged for office-lead approval, not auto-filed. PURCHASED*
Office lead approves the over-budget refill. Claim filed to HR (340 NOK, Priya's employee id, receipt).
HR: approves after 4 days; reimburses Priya in INR (her payroll currency). REIMBURSED

Example B — Bengaluru box (INR budget 15 000/month)

Riya reports box empty. EMPTY
Assignment: Vikram chosen (in Bengaluru today, fewest refills). ASSIGNED
Vikram accepts. COMMITTED
Vikram uploads receipt: “1 200 INR, mixed nuts”. Budget check: 11 800 + 1 200 = 13 000 ≤ 15 000 ✓ → auto-filed to HR. PURCHASED
HR approves; reimburses Vikram in INR. REIMBURSED

3. Parts and the data each keeps

PartRoleData it keeps
Chat tool
(ChatPort, simulated)		The UI. Sends assignment requests, reminders, status updates; receives empty reports, accept/decline, receipt uploads.Nothing durable — it is a transport. (In our build: a simulated stand-in that records sent messages.)
HR system
(HrPort, simulated)		Receives reimbursement claims, returns a claim id, later an approval status. Reimburses in the employee's payroll currency.Its own claim records (HR's concern, not ours).
Core service
(what we build)		Owns the refill state machine, assignment policy, budget check, monthly report.Volunteer (id, home office, current location, refills-done count, last refill date); Refill (id, office, status, owner, deadlines, amount, currency, snack desc, receipt ref, claim id, fx snapshot); Office (budget, budget currency).
Repository
(Repository, in-memory)		Atomic persistence. Status transitions are compare-and-set so a refill is owned by exactly one volunteer.All refill & volunteer records (in-memory in our build).
Clock / IdGen
(ports)		Injectable time and id generation so timeouts and ids are testable.None.

The core depends only on the ports; the chat and HR adapters are swappable stand-ins. In production the chat tool is the UI (a bot in the company's existing chat app) — we never build a separate app. In our deliverable the chat tool is simulated, so the tests themselves drive the lifecycle by calling operations directly.

4. How the problems are prevented

Problem todayHow the design prevents it
Box sits empty for days — everyone assumes someone else will refillEvery refill is assigned to a named person with a deadline; reminders fire before the deadline; on timeout it is reassigned automatically and escalated if nobody is available.
Two people buy on the same dayA refill has a single owner. The atomic claim/commit transition guarantees only one volunteer can be COMMITTED for a refill; a purchase must reference a COMMITTED refill owned by the purchaser.
A few people do most refills; they wait weeks for reimbursementAssignment uses a fairness policy (fewest refills, longest since last, currently at the target office). Reimbursement is filed automatically on receipt upload, removing the manual chase.
Nobody knows monthly spend or which snacks get eatenEvery purchase records amount, currency, snack description. A monthly report totals per office in local currency and gives a combined total in INR using the FX snapshot at purchase date.

5. Trade-offs & what we deliberately don't build

Trade-offs made

  • Human-reported empties over IoT sensors — cheaper, no hardware; mitigated by reminders + deadlines.
  • Manual amount/snack entry over receipt OCR — simpler and accurate; cost is a few seconds of typing.
  • Self-declared availability over calendar integration — no infra dependency; one-tap chat action.
  • FX snapshot at purchase date over live FX — predictable, audit-friendly totals; minor drift acceptable for a snack report.
  • Fairness rotation over optimal buyer — keeps volunteers willing; not always the cheapest/fastest.
  • Over-budget → flag, not auto-file — keeps spending controllable without blocking normal refills.

Deliberately not building

  • IoT box sensors.
  • Receipt OCR / ML.
  • A custom auth system (use existing company SSO).
  • A separate mobile / web app (chat is the UI).
  • A full HR replacement (only the documented claim API).
  • Real-time FX hedging.
  • Cross-office visit scheduling beyond a volunteer's location flag.
Edge cases covered: an employee visiting the other office for a week is eligible only if their current location matches that office; they submit the claim in the purchase currency (HR reimburses in their payroll currency). Hybrid / holiday volunteers are skipped by default but can self-volunteer.

6. Rules the automated tests must prove

  • Two volunteers cannot both commit to the same refill (atomic ownership).
  • A refill nobody completes is reassigned to someone else on timeout.
  • Monthly budgets and totals are correct across both currencies (NOK & INR), including the INR combined total.
